# FIELDPRINT: a time machine for every field, using learned agro-climate fingerprints and causal retrieval of real farms that have already lived your future climate

Every farm field on Earth gets a multimodal foundation-model fingerprint that separates its soil and terrain, the climate it experiences, and how it is managed. FIELDPRINT pushes a field's 2050 climate (NEX-GDDP-CMIP6) into that fingerprint space and retrieves real fields worldwide that already experience that climate on similar soil and terrain. It then uses causal machine learning on 15+ years of satellite-measured outcomes from those fields to estimate which rotation sequences actually worked there, and it says plainly where no evidence exists. It does not look anything up in a table.

## Core idea
The rejected idea was a threshold rule: find months with low NDVI, look up a cover crop, multiply by a constant. FIELDPRINT replaces each of those steps with something learned and causal.

(1) DISENTANGLED FIELD EMBEDDINGS. A multimodal spatiotemporal encoder works at field level, using Fields-of-The-World / USDA Crop Sequence Boundaries polygons. Its backbone is Prithvi-EO-2.0 (600M ViT, pretrained on 4.2M HLS time series, with temporal and location embeddings) for HLS 30m optical time series. A Galileo/Presto-style pixel-timeseries tokenizer handles other inputs: daily forcing tokens (GPM IMERG precipitation, NLDAS/NASA POWER Tmin/Tmax/VPD/radiation), water-state tokens (SMAP L4 root-zone soil moisture, ECOSTRESS LST/ET/ESI, OpenET, GRACE-FO TWS anomaly), static tokens (SRTM/3DEP elevation, slope and profile curvature, SSURGO/gNATSGO texture, AWC, SOM and drainage class) and management tokens (CDL crop history, OpTIS-style winter cover and tillage, HLS-derived residue/NPV fraction). Pretraining combines masked multimodal reconstruction across time and modality with a contrastive objective. The latent space is factorized into three subspaces: S (site: soil and terrain, invariant across years), C (climate: a distributional embedding of the year's forcing, built as a set-transformer or kernel-mean embedding of daily weather, so it encodes timing, extremes and covariance, not just means) and M (management: the rotation and cover sequence). Disentanglement is enforced with adversarial/HSIC independence penalties and a swap test: decoding S from field A with C from year B must reproduce field A's observed response in a year with B-like weather. Because of this factorization, 'this field under 2050 climate' becomes a vector operation: keep S and M, replace C.

(2) FUTURE-CLIMATE PROJECTION INTO EMBEDDING SPACE. NEX-GDDP-CMIP6 daily fields (25 km, many GCMs and SSPs) are downscaled further with the open IBM/NASA Prithvi-WxC downscaler fine-tuned against NASA POWER/IMERG/PRISM and quantile-mapped. They then pass through the same climate encoder used on observed weather. The result is a future C* distribution for each field: an ensemble of points per GCM and SSP, not a single number.

(3) ANALOG RETRIEVAL ACROSS SPACE AND TIME. An approximate-nearest-neighbour index (FAISS-HNSW) covers billions of field-years from 2013-2026 across the US (CDL), Canada (ACI), the EU (EuroCrops/LPIS), NE China, Brazil and Argentina. A query (S_field, C*_2050) returns observed field-years with similar soil and terrain that have already experienced that climate, together with what those farms grew. This extends climate-analogue methods (CCAFS analogues; Fitzpatrick & Dunn 2019), which matched only grid-cell climate, to field-level soil, terrain, management and outcome. When retrieval density is low, a no-analog condition, the system flags novelty (Fitzpatrick & Dunn warn that some future climates have no modern equivalent).

(4) CAUSAL ESTIMATION OF ROTATION EFFECTS ON RETRIEVED ANALOGS. Farmers choose their rotations, so raw outcomes from analog fields are confounded. FIELDPRINT fits orthogonal/double ML and generalized random forests (the grf causal forests used by Deines et al. 2023 and Kluger, Di Tommaso & Lobell 2025), with the S and C embeddings as high-dimensional confounder controls. It estimates conditional average treatment effects (CATE) of a rotation sequence, such as corn-soy-wheat+clover versus corn-soy, on satellite-observed outcomes. Those outcomes are an SCYM-style yield or peak-GCVI proxy, yield in the worst year as a stability measure, ECOSTRESS/OpenET water use per unit yield, bare-soil-days from HLS spectral unmixing (green vegetation, crop residue and bare-soil fractions, not an NDVI threshold), and erosion exposure (bare-soil timing crossed with IMERG storm intensity and SRTM convex curvature, following Thaler et al. 2021, who linked A-horizon loss to curvature). Overlap weighting (ATO) and positivity diagnostics are explicit. If no analog ever ran a candidate rotation in your C*, the effect is not extrapolated. The system reports it as an 'information gap needing local review', which is exactly what the challenge asks for, generated automatically.

(5) WORLD MODEL AND SEQUENCE PLANNING FOR WHAT SATELLITES CANNOT SEE. Soil organic carbon, N2O, nitrate leaching and pest pressure are invisible from orbit. They come from a knowledge-guided ML emulator (KGML-ag-Carbon style, Liu et al. 2024: process-model physics in the architecture, about 10,000x faster than the process model) of Cycles/DayCent/SALUS, conditioned on the field embedding. A sequence model treats rotation as language. It is a decision-transformer / generative world model over tokens of (crop, cover, tillage, weather-year C, outcome) across 8-12-year horizons, pretrained in CyclesGym (a multi-year, multi-crop RL environment) and fine-tuned offline on real CDL×HLS trajectories with conservative offline RL. It searches rotation space, including double crops, relay intercrops and perennial phases, against the whole CMIP6 weather ensemble. The objective is not mean yield but a multi-objective risk measure: profit (USDA AMS/NASS prices), CVaR of yield in bad years, SOC trajectory, water use and erosion. The output is a Pareto frontier. The farmer's priority sliders move a point along that frontier; they do not re-weight hidden rules.

(6) UNCERTAINTY THAT FARMERS CAN READ. Total uncertainty is split into four parts: weather variability (ensemble spread), climate-model disagreement (GCM/SSP spread), model uncertainty (deep-ensemble disagreement plus embedding-density out-of-distribution score) and causal uncertainty (overlap and Rosenbaum/E-value sensitivity to hidden confounders). GeoConformal prediction (distance-weighted conformal) produces calibrated, spatially varying intervals. The map of 'where local expertise must validate' is the map of low overlap and high conformal width. It is computed, not written by hand.

Why this is not if-else: every recommendation is a conditional causal estimate drawn from real outcomes on real analog fields, weighted by learned similarity, propagated through a climate ensemble and calibrated. The same rotation can be recommended in Iowa and rejected in Indiana because the soil-climate interaction differs, and the system can show the evidence.

## Architecture
- L0 Field substrate: field polygons from USDA Crop Sequence Boundaries (US) and the Fields of The World Sentinel-2 boundary model (global, 24 countries), with NAIP 1m for QA. Each field becomes a token sequence on a common daily x field grid. Coarse products (SMAP 9km, IMERG 0.1 deg, GRACE-FO mascons, NEX-GDDP 0.25 deg) are attached through learned area-weighted downscaling heads, not nearest-neighbour resampling.
- L1 Optical/thermal encoder: Prithvi-EO-2.0-600M (HLS L30/S30, 6 bands, multi-temporal) fine-tuned with LoRA into a field-season encoder. Auxiliary self-supervised heads predict spectral-unmixing fractions (green vegetation, crop residue, bare soil) for continuous bare-soil and residue tracking, and ECOSTRESS LST/ET (70m) as a thermal-stress channel. Sentinel-1 SAR fills cloud gaps, Galileo-style.
- L2 Forcing and water-state encoder: set-transformer distributional embedding of daily GPM IMERG precipitation, NLDAS-2/GLDAS/NASA POWER temperature, VPD and radiation, SMAP L4 surface and root-zone soil moisture, the ECOSTRESS Evaporative Stress Index, OpenET ET and GRACE-FO TWS anomaly. Produces the climate subspace C for any year, observed or projected.
- L3 Site encoder: SRTM/3DEP DEM derivatives (slope, profile/plan curvature, TWI, flow accumulation), SSURGO/gNATSGO (and SoilGrids outside the US) texture, AWC, SOM, Ksat and drainage class, plus a GEDI L4A biomass and canopy-structure context for field margins and agroforestry. Produces the site subspace S.
- L4 Management encoder: CDL 2008-2025 crop sequences, OpTIS-style winter cover and tillage detection (re-derived from HLS), and AlphaEarth (GOOGLE_SATELLITE_EMBEDDING_V1_ANNUAL) or TESSERA annual embeddings as auxiliary features. Produces the management subspace M as a sequence.
- L5 Factorized pretraining: masked multimodal autoencoding, cross-modal contrastive alignment, HSIC/adversarial independence between S, C and M, and a counterfactual swap-consistency loss. Output is a 3x128-d FIELDPRINT per field-year.
- L6 Future climate pipeline: NEX-GDDP-CMIP6 (several GCMs, SSP2-4.5/SSP3-7.0/SSP5-8.5), downscaled with the Prithvi-WxC downscaler fine-tuned to NASA POWER/IMERG/PRISM, quantile-delta-mapped, and encoded by the L2 encoder into an ensemble of C* per field for 2030/2040/2050.
- L7 Analog retrieval engine: FAISS-HNSW over all field-years (US, Canada ACI, EuroCrops, NE China, Brazil). Query = (S_field, C*_t). Returns k analog field-years with similarity weights and a novelty score (kNN density); no-analog climates are flagged.
- L8 Causal effect layer: double ML / grf causal forests and DR-learner on analog sets, with outcomes from satellites. These are an SCYM-style Landsat/HLS yield and peak GCVI calibrated to NASS county yields, interannual yield CVaR, ECOSTRESS/OpenET water productivity, HLS bare-soil-days, and an erosion-exposure index (bare-soil fraction x IMERG 30-min storm erosivity x SRTM curvature). Overlap weighting and positivity diagnostics are included, plus E-value sensitivity analysis.
- L9 Process-aware world model: a KGML emulator of Cycles/DayCent/SALUS for SOC, N2O, nitrate leaching and deep drainage, plus a decision-transformer rotation planner pretrained in CyclesGym and fine-tuned with conservative offline RL on real CDL x HLS trajectories. Plans 8-12-year sequences, including double crops, relay intercrops and perennial phases, across the CMIP6 ensemble and returns a Pareto frontier over profit (USDA AMS Market News, NASS QuickStats), yield CVaR, SOC, water and erosion.
- L10 Uncertainty and trust layer: deep-ensemble plus conformal (GeoConformal distance-weighted) intervals, decomposed into weather, climate-model, model and causal parts. Maps of 'local validation required' come from low overlap and high interval width.
- L11 Human loop: farmer-facing 'analog stories' (HLS time-lapse of a real analog farm with its rotation and outcomes) delivered by web map, SMS or voice in a low-literacy mode. Farmer corrections, soil tests, CoCoRaHS rain gauges and USGS NWIS stream nitrate data return as labels. A value-of-information active learner chooses which fields or questions most reduce uncertainty.
- L12 Validation harness: hindcast tests that freeze knowledge in year T and predict rotation effects in T+1..T+5 (for example 2012 drought and 2019 wet-spring prevented planting), using spatial block cross-validation to avoid autocorrelation leakage, and benchmarking against long-term rotation experiments (the 11 sites in Bowles et al. 2020, the KBS LTER).

## Impacts
- **Stop recommending cover crops where they lose money, and show where they win. Turns a one-size practice with a known average yield penalty into field-specific targeting.** (Midwest corn and soybean growers, NRCS/EQIP and state cost-share programs, agricultural lenders): Causal forest CATE on analog fields identifies where covers carry a penalty (good soils, cool mid-season, dry springs for maize) and where they help. Program dollars and farmer trials go to positive-CATE fields, and the system suggests management fixes (termination timing, species) where CATE is negative.. Scale: Deines et al. found an average maize loss of 5.5% (soy 3.5%) on 90k+ fields that used covers for 3+ years, with strong heterogeneity. On US cover-crop acreage (5.9% of row-crop acres in 2021 per OpTIS), recovering even half the penalty on mistargeted acres is worth hundreds of millions of dollars a year. More importantly, it removes the main reason farmers abandon covers, on the path from about 6% adoption to 20-30%.. Evidence: Deines et al. 2023, Global Change Biology 29(3):794-807, doi:10.1111/gcb.16489. OpTIS/CTIC adoption data: https://ctic.org/news/new-optis-data-from-satellite-imagery-show-2-5x-rise-in-corn-belt-cover-crops/
- **Build rotations resilient to drought and heat for the climate a field will have in 10-25 years, not the one it had.** (Farmers in regions shifting toward hotter or drier regimes (western Corn Belt, Great Plains, Mediterranean, North China Plain), crop insurers (RMA), seed companies): Analog retrieval in the C* (2040/2050) embedding finds fields already living that climate. Causal estimates of rotation effects on yield CVaR show which sequences reduced bad-year losses. The world model plans the transition path.. Scale: Bowles et al. 2020 (11 long-term North American sites, 347 site-years): diversified rotations raised maize yields 28.1% on average and 14-90% under adverse and drought conditions. Kluger et al. 2025 (four breadbasket countries): median net precrop benefit 4.5% of yield, larger under rainier conditions, with legume precrop benefits smaller under heat. That climate-conditional effect is exactly what a static tool misses.. Evidence: Bowles et al. 2020, One Earth 2(3):284-293, doi:10.1016/j.oneear.2020.02.007. Kluger, Di Tommaso & Lobell 2025, arXiv:2506.02384
- **Target erosion control at the hilltops where topsoil is actually disappearing, timed to the bare-soil windows that coincide with high-intensity storms.** (Farmers on rolling Corn Belt and Palouse landscapes, downstream water utilities, the Gulf hypoxia program): The erosion-exposure index (HLS bare-soil fraction x IMERG storm erosivity x SRTM convex curvature) is learned per field. Rotations and covers that shorten bare windows during the storm season are ranked by modeled soil retained.. Scale: Thaler et al.: 35 +/- 11% of Corn Belt cultivated area has lost its A-horizon, causing about a 6% yield reduction, $2.8 +/- 0.9B a year in losses and 1.4 +/- 0.5 Pg of carbon removed from hillslopes. Even 10% abatement on the targeted convex areas is worth about $0.3B a year.. Evidence: Thaler, Larsen & Yu 2021, PNAS 118(8):e1922375118, doi:10.1073/pnas.1922375118
- **Place legumes and diversify rotations where they raise both soil carbon and yield, and avoid them where the N2O penalty dominates.** (Smallholders and commercial growers in low-N, cereal-dominated, low-SOC systems, carbon-program MRV, national GHG inventories): The KGML emulator, conditioned on the field embedding, predicts SOC and N2O. The meta-analysis moderators (N rate, crop diversity, initial SOC, climate) are learned as continuous interactions, not as rule bins.. Scale: He et al. 2025 (271 studies, 3,160 observations): legume covers raised SOC 5.9%, yield 16.0% and N2O 36.2%, with the largest gains in low-N, low-diversity, low-SOC, humid-warm systems. Yang et al. 2024 (6-year NCP trial): diversified legume rotations raised equivalent yield up to 38%, cut N2O 39%, raised SOC 8% and improved a soil-health index 45%. Regionally that projects to +32% cereal production and +20% farmer income.. Evidence: He et al. 2025, npj Sustainable Agriculture, doi:10.1038/s44264-025-00050-8 (note: the task brief attributed this to Nature Sustainability 2023, which is a different paper, Vendig et al. 2023, doi:10.1038/s41893-023-01131-7). Yang et al. 2024, Nature Communications 15:198, doi:10.1038/s41467-023-44464-9
- **Deliver rotation advice to farmers who have no agronomist and no local trial data, by learning from analog farms on other continents.** (Smallholders in South Asia, sub-Saharan Africa and Latin America, public extension services): Global embeddings need few labels (the AlphaEarth/TESSERA/Presto results show strong sparse-label transfer). An analog farm in Brazil or the North China Plain can stand in for missing local trials. Advice reaches farmers through voice or SMS as 'farms like yours that already face this weather grew X'.. Scale: The PxD Ama Krushi 3-year RCT in Odisha found +1.74% yield, +4.12% harvest, a 10% lower chance of severe crop loss and 26% lower pest and disease losses, with effects concentrated in anomalous-rainfall years. It reached more than 6.8M farmers after government handover, and PxD's own estimate is $12-19 in benefits per $1. Climate-conditional rotation content plausibly raises the effect in exactly the anomalous years where the RCT found impact.. Evidence: Precision Development Odisha RCT working paper: https://precisiondev.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Odisha_RCT_02052025.pdf
- **Credible, satellite-verified counterfactual baselines for soil-carbon and conservation payments, plus a public evidence-gap map that steers where field trials are funded next.** (USDA (EQIP, CSP), carbon and insetting programs, researchers (NASA Acres, LTAR), funders): Analog-based causal baselines replace modeled-only baselines. Positivity and overlap maps show where the world has never tried a rotation under a given climate, and those become ranked priority sites for on-farm trials (value of information).. Scale: Carbon programs currently rely on process models such as CIBO's SALUS and Regrow's DNDC. Adding empirical analog baselines with calibrated (conformal) intervals tightens MRV uncertainty. The evidence-gap map points trial funding at the highest-uncertainty regions.. Evidence: KGML-ag-Carbon: Liu et al. 2024, Nature Communications 15, doi:10.1038/s41467-023-43860-5. GeoConformal: Lou, Luo & Meng, arXiv:2412.08661. CIBO's SALUS approach: https://www.cibotechnologies.com/resources/what-is-crop-simulation

## Demo moment
A judge clicks a real corn-soy field in central Iowa and drags a slider to '2050, SSP3-7.0'. A globe spins, and about 25 real fields light up in north-central Kansas, eastern Nebraska, the Po Valley and northeast China. Each one has a site fingerprint like the Iowa field and already experiences the Iowa field's 2050 climate today. Clicking one plays its 10-year HLS time-lapse: a corn-sorghum-wheat + clover rotation, with the satellite-measured bare-soil days, ECOSTRESS water stress and yield proxy through the 2022 drought shown next to its corn-soy neighbours. The screen then shows three causally estimated strategies (the current rotation and two alternatives) on a Pareto chart of profit, bad-year yield (CVaR), soil carbon, water and erosion, each with a calibrated interval. A grey hatched zone reads: 'no farm on Earth has tried a perennial phase under this climate on these soils, so local review is required.' Finally the judge presses 'hindcast': the model, frozen with only pre-2012 data, ranks rotations for the 2012 drought, and the ranking is compared with what actually happened on those fields. The same evidence then plays as a 30-second voice explanation in plain language: 'farms like yours that already live with this weather did X, and here is what happened to them.'

# SoilBond: a satellite-verified ledger that pays farmers for resilient rotations

A probabilistic field digital twin built on NASA data. It estimates what a farmer did, what it did to the soil, water and yield risk, and what would have happened otherwise, with uncertainty on each. It then routes that one estimate to every payer who values resilience (crop insurers, lenders, USDA EQIP/CSP, watershed nutrient credits, carbon registries, Scope 3 supply-chain programs). It also uses value-of-information math to reduce the uncertainty that stands between the farmer and the payment. Farmers interact through a voice or WhatsApp agent in their own language, and plots are pooled so smallholders in data-poor regions can be paid too.

## Core idea
The rejected idea treated rotation advice as a lookup: bare month X maps to cover crop Y, which maps to benefit Z. SoilBond treats the farm as a problem of estimating effects, quantifying uncertainty and designing payments. It rests on four interlocking mechanisms.

(1) A JOINT POSTERIOR PER FIELD, not a recommendation. For each field-season the system infers p(management, outcomes, counterfactual | all observations).
- Management (cover crop presence, legume or grass group, planting and termination dates, tillage intensity, rotation sequence) comes from geospatial foundation models (Prithvi on HLS, arXiv:2310.18660; Presto pixel time series, arXiv:2304.14065) fine-tuned on OpTIS, CDL, windshield surveys and farmer voice attestations, with calibrated sets from split-conformal prediction.
- Outcomes (change in SOC, N2O, nitrate leaching, infiltration and runoff, erosion, yield) come from a knowledge-guided ML surrogate in the style of KGML-ag-Carbon (Liu et al. 2024, Nat Commun, doi:10.1038/s41467-023-43860-5). It is pretrained on millions of synthetic DayCent/ecosys/APSIM runs across SSURGO soils x climate x rotation and runs about 10,000x faster than process models. It is then assimilated against SMAP soil moisture, ECOSTRESS/OpenET evapotranspiration, HLS-derived GPP/LAI and satellite yield maps using an ensemble Kalman or particle filter. This follows the 'System-of-Systems' framework of Guan et al. 2023 (Earth-Sci Rev 243:104462).
- The counterfactual comes from orthogonal/double machine learning and generalized causal forests on satellite panels of tens of thousands of fields (the Deines et al. 2023 design, doi:10.1111/gcb.16489). It also uses synthetic-control 'dynamic baselines' built from non-adopting look-alike fields matched in the foundation-model embedding space. This addresses the over-crediting failure mode shown for REDD+ baselines by West et al. 2020 (PNAS).

(2) ONE ESTIMATE, MANY PAYMENTS, WITH A CLAIM LEDGER. Each payer needs a different functional of the same posterior:
- Verra VM0042 v2.2 pays on a probability-of-exceedance quantile of the SOC/GHG distribution.
- A crop insurer needs the conditional average treatment effect of rotation diversity on loss probability. Examples are prevented-planting risk (Won et al. 2024 AJAE, doi:10.1111/ajae.12396) and drought yield loss (Bowles et al. 2020 One Earth, doi:10.1016/j.oneear.2020.02.007).
- A lender needs the shift in the revenue tail (CVaR).
- EQIP/CSP and watershed nutrient-credit buyers need benefit-per-dollar scores routed through a HUC-12 hydrologic model (SWAT+).
- A Scope 3 buyer needs a supply-shed inventory change.
A typed claim ledger gives every tonne, kg of nitrogen or basis point of risk reduction a unique owner and claim ID, so the stacked payments never double count.

(3) VALUE-OF-INFORMATION VERIFICATION. Payouts depend on posterior quantiles, so lower uncertainty literally means more money. Bayesian experimental design (expected payout gain per dollar of measurement) decides where to send a soil core (VM0042 measure-and-remeasure), which farmer to ask for a geotagged photo, and where a CoCoRaHS gauge or extra ECOSTRESS acquisition matters most. Single-field SOC change detection is statistically infeasible (Bradford et al. 2023, Geoderma). A covariance-aware POOLING optimizer therefore builds portfolios of fields, such as a cooperative, a watershed or a smallholder village, whose pooled estimate clears a buyer's confidence threshold at minimum sampling cost. Pooling is how 0.5 ha plots in Malawi or Odisha become creditable and insurable.

(4) ENDOGENOUS ROTATION OPTIMIZATION UNDER CLIMATE AND PAYMENTS. Candidate rotation sequences (current; plus cover crop; diversified small-grain/legume; doubled-up legume for smallholders) are evaluated with the surrogate over bias-corrected NEX-GDDP-CMIP6 ensembles (SSP2-4.5 and SSP5-8.5, 2026-2050) using a stochastic weather generator. Monte Carlo tree search or stochastic dynamic programming over multi-year sequences maximizes a farmer-specific objective: expected net income including stacked payments, minus lambda x CVaR5% of income, subject to labor, equipment and water-right constraints elicited in conversation. The payments themselves change which rotation is optimal, and the honest costs are priced in. Examples are the 5.5% average maize yield penalty for long-term cover cropping (Deines 2023) and the +36% N2O from legume covers. That is why the system answers 'which rotation, where, and who pays for it' rather than 'which cover crop for this month'.

## Architecture
- L1 Multi-resolution harmonized data cube with per-pixel uncertainty. Every source is fused onto a 30 m field grid while its native error is kept.
- HLS L30/S30 (30 m, about 2-3 day revisit) and Sentinel-1 SAR (cloud-penetrating, for cover and residue).
- SMAP L4 (9 km) and SMAP/Sentinel-1 SPL2SMAP_S (3 km/1 km), statistically downscaled with a multi-resolution Gaussian process / deep-kernel model constrained by a water-balance residual.
- ECOSTRESS LST/ET (70 m) and OpenET; GPM IMERG Final (0.1 deg, half-hourly); NASA POWER and NLDAS-2/GLDAS forcing.
- GRACE/GRACE-FO mascons for groundwater drawdown context; GEDI L2B/L4A for hedgerow, agroforestry and perennial biomass.
- SRTM/3DEP for RUSLE LS factors and terrain convexity (the Thaler 2021 topsoil-loss signature); SSURGO/gSSURGO (STATSGO2 or global soil grids where SSURGO is absent).
- CDL, NLCD, PRISM, CoCoRaHS, USGS NWIS, NASS QuickStats, USDA AMS prices, and NEX-GDDP-CMIP6.
- L2 Practice perception. A Prithvi (HLS) and Presto (multi-sensor pixel time series) backbone is fine-tuned in a multi-task setup.
- Outputs: field boundaries, crop type, cover-crop presence and functional group (legume vs grass from red-edge/SWIR phenology), planting and termination dates, tillage intensity from NDTI residue (the NASA Acres Kansas HLS approach), and bare-soil exposure days weighted by IMERG rainfall erosivity.
- Split-conformal prediction gives calibrated per-field sets. Low-confidence or 'no data' fields (as in Regrow's verification guidance) are routed to L7 for alternative evidence.
- L3 Outcome twin. A KGML-ag-Carbon-style surrogate (a GRU/transformer with mass-balance and stoichiometric constraints in the loss) is pretrained on synthetic DayCent/ecosys/APSIM/RUSLE2 runs spanning SSURGO x climate x rotation x management, then fine-tuned on flux towers, long-term trials and satellite yield maps.
- Online assimilation uses an EnKF/particle filter over SMAP soil moisture, ECOSTRESS ET, HLS GPP/LAI and GEDI biomass.
- Outputs are posterior distributions of change in SOC, N2O, nitrate leaching, infiltration and runoff, sediment yield, water demand, and yield for each field-season.
- L4 Causal counterfactual engine. Double ML and generalized random forests (causal forests) on satellite panels estimate heterogeneous treatment effects of rotation diversity and cover cropping on yield, drought-year loss, prevented planting and erosion, conditioned on soil, weather and terrain.
- Synthetic-control dynamic baselines come from non-adopter fields matched in embedding space and refreshed every season to track regional trend drift.
- Conformal intervals on the effect estimates, plus placebo and negative-control tests, catch leakage and confounding.
- L5 Climate-scenario and rotation optimizer. NEX-GDDP-CMIP6 ensembles drive the surrogate through a stochastic weather generator, producing 2026-2050 outcome distributions per rotation sequence.
- MCTS or stochastic dynamic programming over multi-year sequences optimizes mean income (including the stacked payments from L6) minus lambda x CVaR, under farmer constraints.
- The output is a Pareto frontier (income, risk, soil, water) with at least three strategies including the current one. Each strategy carries its evidence, benefits, information gaps and 'needs local review' flags derived from posterior width and out-of-distribution scores.
- Maps cover erosion risk, water-stress hotspots (ECOSTRESS ESI plus SMAP anomalies), frost exposure (POWER/NLDAS minimum temperature vs phenology) and pest-cycle interruption (host-crop continuity from the CDL sequence).
- L6 Value-stack router and claim ledger. Posterior functionals are translated into each payer's evidence standard:
- Verra VM0042 v2.2 QA1/QA2 probability-of-exceedance deductions for carbon.
- An actuarial rate filing for a resilience premium discount, the evidence a USDA RMA 508(h) submission or state program (in the style of Iowa's $5/acre and USDA's PCCP) would need.
- A lender risk-rating feed (an FBN/EDF-style 0.25-0.5% rate cut, but priced from the estimated effect on income CVaR).
- EQIP/CSP and watershed nutrient-credit scoring as benefit per dollar via SWAT+ routing to HUC-12 outlets (reverse-auction compatible).
- Scope 3 insetting at supply-shed level (RegenConnect-style).
An append-only claim registry assigns unique IDs and ownership so each unit of benefit is sold once.
- L7 Value-of-information verifier and pooling optimizer. Bayesian experimental design ranks verification actions (soil core, geotagged photo, CoCoRaHS gauge, drone pass, extension-agent visit) by expected marginal payout per dollar.
- Covariance-aware portfolio construction pools fields (co-ops, watersheds, villages) so pooled MRV uncertainty clears buyer thresholds at minimum sampling cost. This is the route by which smallholders become verifiable.
- L8 Farmer and agency interfaces.
- Farmers: a voice and WhatsApp LLM agent with retrieval and tool calls into L3-L7, never free-generated numbers, speaking local languages at low literacy, in the vein of Farmer.Chat and Ama Krushi. It explains three strategies, the money each unlocks, and what it does not know, and it logs farmer attestations as likelihood terms back into L2.
- Extension agents: a map console.
- Policy sandbox: an agent-based adoption model calibrated on PCCP uptake (12.2M acres at $5/acre vs 5M forecast) lets agencies test 'what if the discount were $10 for diversity score at least X?' and see acres, sediment, nitrate, indemnity and fiscal cost.

## Demo moment
A judge drops a pin on an eroding hilltop field in central Iowa. The map shows HLS-detected bare-soil days stacked against IMERG storm erosivity, the SRTM convexity that marks lost topsoil, and the SMAP drydown curve.

The judge says into the phone: 'What if I add cereal rye and a small-grain year?' The voice agent answers, grounded in the twin, and a 'money waterfall' fills live:
- an insurance premium discount from the causal prevented-planting and drought effect;
- a 0.4% lower loan rate from improved income CVaR under SSP5-8.5 in 2040;
- a jump in EQIP ranking from a HUC-12 nitrate benefit-per-dollar estimate;
- a Scope 3 insetting payment;
- carbon credits shown in grey with an uncertainty deduction.
The yield penalty and the N2O debit are subtracted in red, so the net is honest.

The judge taps 'verify'. The value-of-information engine says one $40 soil core plus one farmer photo raises the expected payout by about $X per acre, because the exceedance deduction shrinks. The judge drags the field into a 300-field co-op pool and watches the pooled credible interval drop below the registry threshold.

Then the same pipeline runs on a Malawi village of 0.5 ha plots. A Chichewa voice agent proposes doubled-up pigeonpea-maize, and the village pool qualifies for IMERG/SMAP-indexed drought cover.

The message: the same NASA pixels, a different farmer, and in both cases the farmer gets paid for resilience.

# SoilTwin: a satellite-corrected field digital twin that rehearses 30 years of rotations before the farmer plants one

Every field gets a living, differentiable agroecosystem model whose hidden soil properties are inferred from 10+ years of NASA observations (HLS, SMAP, ECOSTRESS, IMERG, GRACE-FO). It is continuously corrected by ensemble data assimilation, and it searches for adaptive rotation policies across a 35-model NEX-GDDP-CMIP6 future. The tool returns a risk-aware Pareto set of rotations (yield, profit, topsoil, SOC, nitrate, water). For each one it also gives a map of exactly what is still unknown and where one soil core or one farmer observation would change the answer.

## Core idea
The lookup-table approach treats a field as a label ("bare Nov-Apr, so plant rye"). SoilTwin treats a field as a partially observed dynamical system, x_{t+1} = f_theta(x_t, weather_t, management_t) + noise. The state x holds layered soil water, mineral N, LAI/biomass, residue, multi-pool SOC (fast/slow/passive), and the A-horizon depth. The parameters theta are field-specific and mostly invisible from space: layer field capacity, wilting point, Ksat, root-restricting depth, initial SOC pool partition, N-mineralization rate, cover-crop and cash-crop cultivar traits. The recommendation comes from inference and optimisation over this system, not from rules. The technical core has five parts.

(1) Process model plus differentiable KGML surrogate. A process model (APSIM NextGen as primary, with ecosys/DayCent-style SOC-N2O physics as a second structural hypothesis) generates tens of millions of synthetic site-years. Those runs span SSURGO soils, Daymet/NLDAS/NEX-GDDP weather, and a combinatorial management space: crop sequence, cover species/mix, planting and termination dates, tillage, N rate. A knowledge-guided hierarchical GRU / neural-ODE surrogate is pre-trained on them (the KGML-ag recipe, Liu et al. GMD 2022). Its architecture mirrors the process model's causal graph (water -> N -> growth -> residue -> SOC/N2O), so every intermediate state stays physically named and observable. It is then fine-tuned on flux towers, long-term rotation trials and satellite yield. Because the surrogate is differentiable (Shen et al. 2023, Nat Rev Earth Env), gradients flow from satellite misfit back to soil parameters. The surrogate is also roughly 1000x faster, which turns 30-year x 35-GCM x 1000-policy rollouts from cluster-days into seconds and makes live sliders possible.

(2) Two-timescale data assimilation. (a) Reanalysis/inversion over 2013 to present: an iterative ensemble smoother (ES-MDA) or ensemble-variational 4D-Var on the surrogate jointly estimates theta and the initial SOC state. It uses dense HLS 30 m LAI/NDVI time series (including winter greenness, which reveals actual cover-crop history and biomass), SMAP SPL2SMAP_S 3 km and SMAP L4 root-zone soil moisture, ECOSTRESS 70 m ET/ESI and OpenET 30 m ET, and CDL crop sequence. The result is a posterior distribution over the field's soil, not a point estimate. Amortized simulation-based inference (a neural posterior estimator trained on the same simulator) makes per-field posterior inference take milliseconds, so it can scale to every US field. (b) In-season: an EnKF, with a particle filter where state-observation relations are strongly nonlinear (dry-down, water stress), updates soil water, LAI and N states as each overpass arrives. This follows Kivi et al. HESS 2023 (APSIM+EnKF, 17% median yield-RMSE reduction, largest in water-stressed years) and KGML-DA (Yang et al. RSE 2023, surrogate+EnKF, -26.5% corn / -36.2% soy county yield error).

(3) Multi-resolution harmonization done with observation operators. Data are not crudely resampled to one grid. The twin runs at 30 m sub-field resolution, and each sensor gets an observation operator H that maps twin states into that sensor's footprint and physics: area-weighted aggregation to the SMAP 3/9/36 km footprint with representativeness error; a PROSAIL emulator mapping LAI and chlorophyll to HLS reflectance; ET mapped to the ECOSTRESS 70 m swath at its actual overpass time of day; GRACE-FO/GLDAS-DA terrestrial water storage as a ~300 km regional constraint on irrigation-water availability. Mixed pixels, sparse revisits and scale mismatch become explicit error-covariance terms rather than silent bias. This is the principled answer to the brief's 'harmonize onto a common grid.'

(4) Counterfactual policy search under climate futures. The twin's posterior ensemble is crossed with NEX-GDDP-CMIP6 (35 GCMs, SSP2-4.5/SSP5-8.5, field-downscaled with a stochastic weather generator conditioned on PRISM/Daymet) and with candidate management policies. Candidates are not fixed lookup rotations. They are adaptive, state-feedback rules learned with multi-objective evolutionary search (NSGA-II / MAP-Elites) and model-based RL in a gym-DSSAT-style environment wrapped around the surrogate. An example rule: 'terminate cereal rye 10 days early if the April SMAP root-zone percentile is below 30 and the 30-day IMERG anomaly is negative; add wheat into the corn-soy sequence when the twin's projected July heat-stress probability exceeds p.' Objectives are full distributions, optimised with CVaR so tail years (droughts, wet springs) count: yield, net return (NASS QuickStats / AMS prices), SOC trajectory, water erosion (RUSLE2-style plus tillage erosion from 3DEP/SRTM curvature, the convex-hilltop mechanism Thaler et al. PNAS 2021 identified), nitrate leaching, N2O, and irrigation/water demand.

(5) Uncertainty that produces action. A Sobol/Shapley decomposition splits each recommendation's outcome variance into climate, soil-parameter, model-structure (APSIM vs ecosys-style surrogate disagreement) and execution sources. An expected-value-of-sample-information calculation then answers the brief's 'information gaps / needs local review' with specifics. Example: '68% of the uncertainty about whether rye costs you corn yield comes from subsoil water-holding capacity in the NE corner; one 1 m soil core at this GPS point has the highest expected decision value.' Farmer knowledge also enters as assimilated observations, not just UI comments ('ponded in May 2019', 'tile-drained', 'yield was 180'). The twin therefore supports farmer judgment rather than replacing it.

A population-level causal check closes the loop. The twin's predicted cover-crop yield effects across thousands of fields are calibrated, through hierarchical Bayesian priors, against observational satellite causal estimates: the causal-forest result of Deines et al. GCB 2023 of -5.5% maize / -3.5% soy, with heterogeneity by soil, spring rain and June heat. This keeps mechanistic simulation honest against real-world outcomes.

## Architecture
- L0 Field geometry and priors: CLU/field boundaries; SSURGO/gSSURGO horizons, mapped to pedotransfer-function priors on the soil-hydraulic and SOC parameters theta; USGS 3DEP/SRTM DEM giving slope, curvature and flow accumulation (tillage-erosion and water-erosion drivers); Cropland Data Layer 2008-present as the cash-crop sequence prior; NLCD/LANDFIRE masks; Web Soil Survey K-factor and T-value.
- L1 Forcing: GPM IMERG Final (0.1 deg, 30 min) merged with PRISM/Daymet and CoCoRaHS gauges through a Bayesian precipitation-error model; NASA POWER and NLDAS-2 radiation, temperature, humidity and wind; forcing uncertainty is carried as an ensemble rather than a single trace.
- L2 Process models (the physics): APSIM NextGen (crop, water, N, SoilTemp, SurfaceOrganicMatter, cover-crop modules) as the primary model, with ecosys/DayCent-style SOC-N2O as a second structural hypothesis. Tens of millions of synthetic site-years run across the SSURGO x weather x management design space, using Latin hypercube plus active-learning sampling concentrated where the surrogate is uncertain.
- L3 Differentiable KGML surrogate: a hierarchical GRU / neural-ODE whose module boundaries mirror the process causal graph (water balance -> N cycling -> phenology/growth -> residue -> SOC pools/N2O), pre-trained on L2 output and fine-tuned on AmeriFlux/LTAR flux towers, the Bowles et al. long-term rotation trials and SCYM satellite yields. Implemented in JAX/PyTorch for autodiff. A deep-ensemble/SWAG head provides surrogate epistemic uncertainty, and an out-of-distribution detector falls back to full APSIM when inputs leave the training envelope.
- L4 Observation operators H: SMAP SPL2SMAP_S (3 km) and SMAP L4 root-zone soil moisture via footprint aggregation plus representativeness error; HLS L30/S30 reflectance via a PROSAIL emulator (LAI, Cab, fCover), including winter-season cover-crop detection and biomass (Thieme/Xu HLS biomass models); ECOSTRESS L3 ET (PT-JPL/DisALEXI) and ESI at 70 m and OpenET 30 m, matched to overpass time and day; MODIS/VIIRS 8-day LAI/FPAR and LST to fill gaps; GEDI L4A biomass for perennial and woody edge features; GRACE-FO mascons via GLDAS-CLSM GRACE-DA groundwater (Li et al. WRR 2019) as a regional water-availability constraint for irrigated or deficit scenarios.
- L5 Assimilation engine: (a) offline reanalysis, using amortized neural posterior estimation for a fast first guess, then ES-MDA / EnVar 4D-Var on the differentiable surrogate to get the joint posterior of soil parameters theta and the initial SOC partition from 2013-present; (b) online EnKF (Kalman) / SIR particle filter (nonlinear stress periods) state updates at each SMAP/HLS/ECOSTRESS overpass; (c) farmer observations (flooding, yields, tile drainage, planting dates) entered as low-rank observations with elicited error variance.
- L6 Climate futures: NEX-GDDP-CMIP6 daily 0.25 deg (35 GCMs, SSP2-4.5/3-7.0/5-8.5, 2026-2060), field-downscaled with a conditional stochastic weather generator trained on PRISM/IMERG to restore extremes and spell lengths; NOAA/NMME subseasonal forecasts for the coming season; GCM weighting by skill on the field's historical climate.
- L7 Policy search: the management space is encoded as a grammar (crop sequence x cover species/mix x plant/terminate windows x tillage x N rate x state-triggered rules). Multi-objective search uses NSGA-II / MAP-Elites over open-loop rotations plus model-based RL (PPO on a gym-DSSAT-style wrapper of the surrogate) for closed-loop adaptive policies. The objectives are CVaR_10% and mean of yield, net return (NASS QuickStats, AMS Market News, NRCS EQIP/CSP payments), SOC change at 0-30 and 30-100 cm, RUSLE2 + tillage erosion against the soil T-value, nitrate leaching, N2O, water use, and income diversification. The farmer's current rotation is always included as the baseline.
- L8 Uncertainty and value of information: Sobol/Shapley variance attribution (climate / soil parameters / model structure / execution); expected value of sample information over candidate soil-core locations and farmer questions; robustness flags where the Pareto ranking flips across GCMs or across APSIM vs ecosys-style structures, marked 'needs local agronomist review'.
- L9 Population learning and causal calibration: hierarchical Bayesian priors on theta pooled across thousands of fields per MLRA; calibration of twin-predicted practice effects against satellite causal-forest estimates (Deines et al. 2023) and NASA Acres/OpTIS practice maps; each season's realized outcomes (SCYM yield, HLS cover biomass) update the regional priors, so the system improves every year.
- L10 Farmer interface: preference elicitation through pairwise 'which future do you prefer' comparisons, learning utility weights (Bradley-Terry) rather than asking for numeric weights; 'season ribbon' timelines showing bare-soil, vulnerability and pest-cycle windows emerging from simulated states; three to five Pareto rotations each explained by the twin's mechanism trace (why), evidence links, benefits, information gaps (from L8) and local-review risks; voice and SMS delivery in plain language (Odisha-style advisory); the farmer can override any step, and the override is logged as an observation.

# ROOTS: Robust Optimization Of Transition Strategies (regret-aware rotation pathways for a changing climate)

ROOTS treats a farm's next 10 years as a sequential decision problem in which the field's condition is only partly visible. A satellite-fed digital twin keeps a probability distribution over each field's hidden soil state, and a risk-aware multi-objective planner searches thousands of climate, price and model futures. The output is not one 'best rotation' but a few contingent pathways with signposts ('if X happens, switch to Y'), the farmer's own risk tolerance built in, and a dollar value on which soil test or observation would most reduce the chance of a regretted decision.

## Core idea
The rejected approach ('find bare months, look up a cover crop') assumes the field state, the future and the farmer's goals are all known. Under that assumption a lookup table is enough. ROOTS drops all three assumptions and builds the method around that.

(1) Field state is partially observed, so the problem is a POMDP. The hidden state of each field polygon (USDA Crop Sequence Boundaries) is s_t = {soil organic carbon (SOC) by depth, plant-available water capacity, infiltration/compaction index, residual nitrate, fraction of exposed subsoil from erosion, weed/pathogen carryover from crop history, groundwater access}. We never observe s_t directly. A hierarchical Bayesian state-space model keeps a belief b_t(s), represented by a particle filter, and updates it every season from satellite observations (HLS reflectance and Prithvi-EO embeddings, SMAP L4 root-zone soil moisture, ECOSTRESS and OpenET evapotranspiration and LST, GPM IMERG rainfall, Landsat bare-soil composites) plus whatever the farmer adds (a soil test, a yield-monitor file). Slow variables such as SOC stay uncertain and say so; fast variables such as soil moisture and green cover get pinned down quickly. The tool's 'uncertainty' is this posterior, not a decorative error bar.

(2) Dynamics come from a hybrid model checked against causal estimates. A process-model ensemble (Cycles, DSSAT/APSIM, DayCent) is run millions of times across SSURGO soils x NEX-GDDP-CMIP6 weather x candidate rotations, then distilled into a fast neural sequence emulator. A causal-forest residual model trained on observational data (CSB 8-year rotation histories x HLS-derived yields, following Deines et al. 2023 on 90k fields) corrects the emulator's systematic bias. Disagreement between process models is kept as an explicit dimension of deep uncertainty. It is never averaged away.

(3) The objectives are a vector, so the output is a Pareto set. r_t = (net income, ΔSOC, soil loss including tillage erosion on convex hilltops, consumptive water use, N2O, nitrate leaching). Pareto Conditioned Networks (Reymond et al. 2022) learn one policy network conditioned on a target return vector, so the whole frontier is available instantly when the farmer moves a slider. Choosing a weighted-sum point would hide trade-offs such as legume covers adding SOC and yield while raising N2O by about 36%.

(4) Risk is handled with nested (time-consistent) CVaR. Farmers do not maximise expected profit. Survey evidence shows economic return and first-year downside risk are what block adoption. We optimise CVaR_α of the farmer's utility over the rotation horizon. Chow et al. (2015) show that a CVaR MDP is equivalent to robustness against a bounded model-error budget, so one dial covers both 'risk aversion' and 'distrust of the model'. The farmer's α and objective weights are learned from about 8 pairwise 'which future would you rather have' cards using Bayesian preference exploration (BOPE, Lin et al. 2022), with the current rotation's income as the prospect-theory reference point.

(5) Deep uncertainty is handled with Robust Decision Making (RDM) and adaptation pathways. Candidate policies are stress-tested across about 10^4 futures (35 CMIP6 GCMs x 4 SSPs x price regimes x policy and insurance regimes x model-structure weights). Regret = performance shortfall against the best policy in hindsight for that future. PRIM scenario discovery (Lempert/Bryant) then finds the few readable conditions in which each pathway fails, e.g. 'the rye-before-soy pathway fails mainly when April is both cold and dry'. These failure boxes become Dynamic Adaptive Policy Pathways (Haasnoot) tipping points, with satellite-observable signposts such as 'SMAP April root-zone moisture below the 20th percentile in 2 of 3 years → branch to pathway B'. The recommendation is therefore a contingent plan the satellites keep monitoring, not a one-time answer.

(6) Information gaps are quantified with value of information. For each possible action to gather information (a $30 SOC/texture test at a specific point, a Haney test, a soil-moisture probe, uploading yield-monitor data, simply waiting one season), ROOTS computes the expected value of sample information (EVSI) as the expected reduction in decision regret, by nested Monte Carlo over the belief. It also chooses the sampling location through Bayesian experimental design on the spatial posterior. The brief asks for 'information gaps and risks needing local review'; ROOTS answers with a ranked list priced in dollars. Where model-structure disagreement dominates the posterior, the field is flagged for local agronomist review instead of being recommended confidently.

None of this is an if-else rule. The recommendation comes out of belief-space planning: change the farmer's risk dial, the soil test result or the climate scenario and the optimal contingent policy changes in non-obvious ways.

## Architecture
- L0 Common analysis grid and field ontology: USDA NASS Crop Sequence Boundaries polygons (8-yr CDL rotation history) on a 30 m HLS grid. Every input carries its own error model so uncertainty can propagate through the later layers.
- L1 Multi-resolution fusion, with ensembles instead of single values: SMAP L4 9 km root-zone soil moisture downscaled to 30 m using ECOSTRESS LST/ET, HLS NDVI and SSURGO texture through a conditional generative downscaler (e.g. GP or diffusion) that outputs 50-member ensembles. GPM IMERG merged with PRISM/CoCoRaHS by Bayesian kriging. NLDAS-2/NASA POWER forcing. OpenET ensemble ET (cropland MAE about 17%) as an observation of water use. GRACE/GRACE-FO groundwater anomalies as a regional constraint on irrigation availability. SRTM/3DEP curvature plus Landsat bare-soil composites to map exposed subsoil (Thaler 2021 method). Prithvi-EO-2.0 (HLS foundation model) fine-tuned to detect cover, residue and bare-soil days. GEDI L4A biomass for field-edge woody options (buffers, agroforestry).
- L2 Belief-state digital twin: a hierarchical Bayesian state-space model with a particle filter (or EnKF for continuous states) over SOC, PAWC, infiltration, residual N, erosion-exposed fraction, and pest/pathogen carryover inferred from the CSB crop sequence. SSURGO/STATSGO2 and SoilGrids act as priors, satellite series as the likelihood, and optional farmer soil tests as high-weight observations.
- L3 Hybrid world model: a process-model ensemble (Cycles, as used in CyclesGym, plus DSSAT/APSIM and DayCent for C/N/N2O) run over SSURGO soils x NEX-GDDP-CMIP6 x rotations, distilled into a neural sequence emulator (transformer/neural-operator surrogate) that runs about 10^5 seasons per second on GPU. A causal-forest residual correction is trained on observational CSB x HLS-yield panels (Deines et al. 2023 design). Model-structure weights stay explicit as an uncertainty dimension.
- L4 Futures generator for deep uncertainty: Latin-hypercube sampling over 35 CMIP6 GCMs x SSP1-2.6/2-4.5/3-7.0/5-8.5, a stochastic weather generator conditioned on each GCM, price regimes from USDA AMS Market News and NASS QuickStats with commodity-volatility shocks, policy regimes (EQIP/CSP payments, insurance discounts, a possible carbon price), and model-structure weights. Roughly 10^4 futures in total.
- L5 Risk-aware multi-objective POMDP planner: belief-space Monte Carlo tree search (POMCP/DESPOT family) over the emulator, with actions = {cash crop, cover species or mix, seeding and termination date, tillage, N rate, irrigation}. Pareto Conditioned Networks amortise the frontier over (income, ΔSOC, erosion, water, N2O, NO3 leaching), and nested CVaR_α Bellman backups (Chow et al. 2015) make the plans robust to risk and model error.
- L6 RDM stress test and pathways: regret computed per future for each candidate policy, PRIM scenario discovery to extract plain-language failure boxes, and a Dynamic Adaptive Policy Pathways 'metro map' whose tipping points are tied to satellite signposts (SMAP spring moisture anomaly, ECOSTRESS Evaporative Stress Index, GRACE groundwater trend, IMERG spring rain) that ROOTS keeps monitoring each season.
- L7 Preference and value-of-information engine: BOPE pairwise-card elicitation of objective weights, CVaR α and reference point. EVSI/EVPPI for each possible measurement through nested Monte Carlo over the belief. Bayesian optimal experimental design picks where in the field to take the soil core. A determinantal-point-process diversity selector picks the 3 recommended pathways to be Pareto-distinct and close to the farmer's preferences, shown next to the existing rotation (as the challenge requires).
- L8 Farmer-facing and landscape outputs: the existing rotation plus 3 pathway cards, each with why it was chosen (counterfactual explanation), evidence (the citations and the field's own satellite history), benefits, risk shown as natural frequencies ('in 1 of 10 years you'd be about $38/ac worse off'), information gaps ranked by EVSI in dollars, and 'needs local review' flags where epistemic variance dominates. Delivered as voice/SMS for low-literacy users. A landscape layer runs uplift modelling across all CSB fields to show conservation agencies and insurers where a dollar of incentive buys the most additional practice change.

## Impacts
- **Lower drought and bad-year losses through risk-targeted rotation diversification** (Corn Belt and Great Plains grain farmers, crop insurers (RMA) and their indemnity budgets): The nested-CVaR planner rewards diversity because it cuts losses in the lower tail, not just the mean. The pathways show the farmer, for their own field, how much a diversified rotation narrows the bad-year distribution. That turns a long-term benefit with no visible payoff into a risk-management choice the farmer can see.. Scale: In long-term trials at 11 North American sites, more diverse rotations raised maize yields by about 28% on average and cut drought-year yield losses by 14-90%. Applied to even a few percent of roughly 90M US corn acres, the avoided tail losses are worth hundreds of millions of dollars in dry years (order-of-magnitude extrapolation, needs validation).. Evidence: Bowles et al. 2020, One Earth 2(3):284-293, doi:10.1016/j.oneear.2020.02.007; summary https://www.unh.edu/unhtoday/2020/03/rotating-crops-over-time-boosts-corn-yields-even-droughts
- **Fewer cover-crop failures and less disadoption, by forecasting each field's yield penalty instead of recommending cover crops everywhere** (First-time cover-crop adopters (the 60% of non-users who cite economic return as the barrier), extension agents): The causal-forest-corrected world model predicts heterogeneous treatment effects. Deines et al. found larger maize penalties on better soils, in cooler mid-seasons and in drier springs. ROOTS steers those fields to lower-risk species, earlier termination or a different pathway, and its signposts warn when a dry spring raises the risk.. Scale: Satellite causal-forest analysis of more than 90,000 fields found an average maize yield loss of 5.5% (3.5% for soybean) where cover crops had been used for 3+ years. Cutting that penalty on the roughly 5% of US cropland now in cover crops, and preventing the disadoption it causes, is the lever that could take adoption beyond the single digits.. Evidence: Deines et al. 2023, Global Change Biology 29(3):794-807, doi:10.1111/gcb.16489; SARE/CTIC 2022-23 National Cover Crop Survey https://www.sare.org/wp-content/uploads/2022-2023-National-Cover-Crop-Survey-Report.pdf
- **Rebuild topsoil where it was lost by targeting erosion-reducing pathways (no-till plus living roots, perennial strips) at eroded hilltops** (Landowners on rolling Corn Belt terrain, downstream water utilities, the long-run productivity of farmland): SRTM/3DEP curvature plus bare-soil composites mark exposed subsoil in the belief state. The erosion objective includes tillage erosion, which NRCS models miss. The Pareto frontier shows the income-versus-soil trade-off down to the sub-field zone.. Scale: 35 ± 11% of cultivated Corn Belt area (about 34M of about 96M acres studied) has lost its A-horizon, costing roughly 6% of yield, or $2.8 ± 0.9B per year. ROOTS can map this target area directly.. Evidence: Thaler, Larsen & Yu 2021, PNAS 118(8):e1922375118, doi:10.1073/pnas.1922375118
- **Climate-honest practice choice: capture SOC and yield gains without unknowingly raising N2O** (Farmers in carbon or insetting programs, food companies with Scope 3 targets, MRV providers): Because N2O is a separate objective, ROOTS will not recommend a legume cover in high-N, humid settings unless the plan also lowers synthetic N or adds no-till. Single-metric carbon tools miss this trade-off.. Scale: A meta-analysis of 271 studies (3,160 observations) found legume cover crops raise SOC by 5.9% and yield by 16.0% but N2O by 36.2%. Gains were largest under low N inputs, low crop diversity and low initial SOC, which is exactly where ROOTS' belief state points it.. Evidence: 'Optimizing cover cropping application for sustainable crop production', npj Sustainable Agriculture 2025 (record: https://researchers.une.edu.au/en/publications/optimizing-cover-cropping-application-for-sustainable-crop-produc/). Note: the brief attributed these numbers to Nature Sustainability 2023; that paper is Vendig et al. 2023 (SOC and yield, not N2O): https://food.berkeley.edu/from-the-field/the-dual-benefit-of-cover-cropping-soil-carbon-and-increased-crop-yields/
- **More conservation per public dollar by targeting incentives with uplift modelling** (NRCS (EQIP/CSP), state agencies such as the Iowa Department of Agriculture and Land Stewardship (IDALS), the USDA Risk Management Agency (RMA), and taxpayers): The landscape layer estimates, for each CSB field, how likely an incentive is to change the farmer's optimal CVaR policy (additionality) and what that change achieves on each objective. It then ranks fields by marginal benefit per dollar, as Deines et al. suggested for targeting subsidies.. Scale: Iowa's flat $5/acre insurance discount now covers more than 1M acres, but survey evidence suggests only about 11% of enrolled area is additional, and focus groups found $5 too small to change behaviour. Even moderate targeting gains could double the number of additional acres per program dollar (hypothesis to test).. Evidence: Sawadgo 2024, Agricultural & Environmental Letters 9:e20125, doi:10.1002/ael2.20125; Irvine et al. stakeholder study https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10638564
- **Cheaper, better-targeted soil testing: ROOTS tells the farmer which test, where, and whether it is worth paying for** (Farmers, crop consultants, soil labs, carbon project developers (sampling is the main MRV cost)): EVSI ranks each possible measurement by the expected regret it removes, and Bayesian experimental design picks the sampling locations. - **Water-secure rotations where aquifers are depleting** (Irrigators on the High Plains, Central Valley and Lower Mississippi, groundwater districts): OpenET/ECOSTRESS observed water use calibrates the water objective. GRACE-FO groundwater trends act as both a scenario constraint and a signpost, so pathways shift to lower-ET crops or dryland rotations before the aquifer forces the change.. Scale: OpenET's ensemble reaches 15.8 mm/month MAE (17%) at cropland flux sites (r² = 0.9), accurate enough to make a field-scale water objective credible for millions of irrigated acres in the western US.. - **Advice that reaches farmers at scale through low-literacy voice delivery** (Smallholders and low-literacy farmers (the global extension of the tool)): Pathway cards and signposts reduce to short voice messages in the local language ('this spring is dry: keep plan B'), the delivery channel already proven in phone-based advisory RCTs.. Scale: A phone-based advisory RCT in Odisha (13,675 rice farmers, yields measured by remote sensing) found modest yield gains and better resilience to weather shocks, delivered at very low marginal cost per farmer.. Evidence: Cole, Goldberg, Harigaya & Zhu 2025, PxD Odisha RCT https://precisiondev.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Odisha_RCT_02052025.pdf

# COUNTERFIELD: a counterfactual twin for every U.S. field, built from 17 years of natural experiments seen by NASA satellites

Treat 16 million U.S. fields, each with a mapped crop sequence since 2008, as one large natural experiment. COUNTERFIELD uses causal machine learning to estimate what each field's yield, water balance, soil carbon and drought resilience would have been under a different rotation. It shows the farmer the real 'statistical twin' fields that already made that switch, states how sure the estimate is, and marks where local trials are still needed. It also projects those effects into CMIP6 future climates.

## Core idea
Recommending a rotation is a causal question, not a classification task. The answer we want is the field's yield, water balance, soil carbon and drought behaviour under a rotation it did not grow. You cannot read that off a lookup table or a correlation. Fields that already rotate differ from fields that don't (soil, drainage, how the operator manages, landscape position), so 'diverse-rotation fields yield more' is confounded. COUNTERFIELD frames the whole problem as estimating heterogeneous, dynamic treatment effects from observational Earth data. Five pieces fit together.

(1) THE TREATMENT IS A MULTI-YEAR REGIME, NOT A YES/NO. Rotation is a dynamic treatment regime. Last year's crop changes this year's soil moisture, residue and pest pressure, and those in turn influence what the farmer plants next. They are time-varying confounders that are themselves affected by earlier treatment, so a naive regression is biased. We estimate regime effects such as 'corn-soy-wheat plus clover for 4 years' versus 'continuous corn' with Double/Debiased ML for dynamic treatment effects. This is cross-fitted, orthogonal g-estimation (Lewis and Syrgkanis, NeurIPS 2021, arXiv:2002.07285). Sequences are encoded as learned embeddings of the 2008-2025 CDL/Crop Sequence Boundaries history. Bare-soil exposure is treated as a continuous dose: bare days weighted by IMERG rainfall erosivity, from HLS NDVI/NDTI time series. It is not an if-else flag.

(2) THREE IDENTIFICATION STRATEGIES THAT CHECK EACH OTHER. (a) Staggered difference-in-differences and event studies (Callaway and Sant'Anna 2021) on fields that switched rotation in different years, compared against not-yet-switched fields under the same weather. (b) Generalized synthetic control (Xu 2017) per target field. The donor pool is matched on the pre-switch trajectory of Prithvi-EO-2.0 HLS embeddings, SSURGO soil properties, SRTM/3DEP terrain and NASA POWER/PRISM weather. This produces a literal counterfactual time series for THIS field, assembled from real fields that switched. (c) Causal forests and R-learners (Wager and Athey 2018; Chernozhukov et al. 2018) that estimate the conditional average treatment effect (CATE) as a function of soil, terrain and growing-season weather. Including weather as an effect modifier is what allows transport into future climates. The three estimators must agree before the tool shows any recommendation. Where they disagree, the disagreement itself is shown as an information gap.

(3) DIRECT ATTACKS ON THE ERRORS THAT USUALLY BREAK SATELLITE CAUSAL STUDIES. Spatial confounding (unmeasured, spatially structured drivers such as tile drainage or local agronomist networks) is handled four ways. We use spatially blocked cross-fitting in DML and spatial basis functions as nuisance covariates (Gilbert et al., arXiv:2112.14946). We fix effects at the operator-cluster level using contiguous CSB parcels. We run negative-control outcomes, for example pre-switch yields and pre-switch soil-moisture drydown, which a later rotation cannot have caused. Measurement error in CDL labels and SCYM-style satellite yields would otherwise attenuate effects. We correct it with prediction-powered inference (Angelopoulos et al., Science 2023): a small gold-standard sample (RMA/yield-monitor data, LTAR and long-term trials) debiases the effect estimated from 16M proxy-labelled fields. We also pool with randomized trials using the hybrid estimator of Kluger, Owen and Lobell (arXiv:2112.13700), which gave 13% lower RMSE than trials alone and 26% lower than satellites alone. Finally, a conformal sensitivity analysis (Jin, Ren and Candès, arXiv:2111.12161) reports a 'flip strength': how strong an unmeasured confounder would have to be to reverse the recommendation.

(4) HONEST PER-FIELD UNCERTAINTY AND EXPLICIT GAPS. We build individual-level prediction intervals with weighted conformal inference for counterfactuals (Lei and Candès, JRSS-B 2021), which is doubly robust under ignorability. Positivity and overlap diagnostics count how many genuinely similar fields actually tried the alternative. Where overlap collapses (diverse rotations are rare in parts of the Corn Belt), the estimate is not hidden. It is hatched and labelled 'beyond the evidence – recommend a strip trial', and those labelled fields become the information gaps and local-review flags the challenge brief asks for.

(5) FROM EFFECTS TO DECISIONS. Each farmer's priorities (income, drought insurance, soil, water, labour) set the weights of a multi-objective utility. For every candidate regime (the status quo plus at least 3 alternatives) we integrate the CATE over that field's NEX-GDDP-CMIP6 2035-2055 weather distribution. Where future weather falls outside the historical support, we flag the extrapolation instead of reporting a number. The output is a Pareto front, not a single answer. At the landscape level, Athey-Wager policy learning (Econometrica 2021) produces shallow, interpretable policy trees. They state which kinds of fields should be offered which rotation or cover crop cost-share to get the most soil and water benefit per dollar. That turns field-level counterfactuals into conservation targeting.

## Architecture
- L0 Field universe and treatment history. USDA NASS Crop Sequence Boundaries (about 16M CONUS field polygons, 2008-present) joined to the annual Cropland Data Layer. Each field-year gets a learned sequence embedding (transformer over crop tokens). Winter cover and bare-soil periods come from HLS L30/S30 NDVI, GCVI and NDTI time series at 30 m, every 2-3 days. Cover detection is cross-checked against OpTIS/Regrow-style winter-greenness detection. The bare-soil dose is the sum of bare days times daily GPM IMERG rainfall erosivity.
- L1 Common grid and confounder stack (all harmonized to the 30 m field polygon by area-weighted aggregation, with uncertainty propagated). SSURGO/gSSURGO (AWC, Ksat, OM, drainage class, NCCPI). SRTM and 3DEP terrain (slope, curvature, which Thaler et al. 2021 link to A-horizon loss, and topographic wetness). NASA POWER, PRISM and gridMET daily weather. Pre-period Prithvi-EO-2.0 (NASA/IBM, arXiv:2412.02732) HLS embeddings as a high-dimensional summary of management and soil-surface state. GRACE/GRACE-FO groundwater anomaly as a regional water-scarcity modifier. NLCD/LANDFIRE landscape context.
- L2 Outcome sensors (multi-objective, all satellite-derived). (a) Yield: SCYM-style scalable crop yield mapper (Lobell et al. 2015, RSE) on HLS plus weather, calibrated to NASS QuickStats county yields and debiased with PPI against RMA/yield-monitor gold data. (b) Water: ECOSTRESS 70 m ET, ESI and LST, plus OpenET 30 m ET for water use and heat stress. (c) Infiltration and storage signature: SMAP-HydroBlocks 30 m soil moisture (Vergopolan et al. 2021) and SMAP L4 root-zone moisture, combined with IMERG storm events to derive per-field storm-response gain and drydown timescale. Better soil structure shows up as more water stored and slower drydown. (d) Soil carbon trend: Landsat/HLS bare-soil reflectance composites since 1986 (Broeg et al. 2024, GCB). Only the direction of the trend is used, because trend magnitude validates poorly (R²≈0.16). (e) Erosion exposure: a RUSLE-style C-factor from the bare-soil dose, IMERG erosivity and LS factor. (f) Resilience: yield anomaly in IMERG/ECOSTRESS-defined drought or excess-wet years. (g) Above-ground biomass anchors from GEDI L4A footprints for cover-crop and residue biomass calibration where footprints intersect fields.
- L3 Causal core. (i) Dynamic DML / g-estimation for multi-year regimes (Lewis and Syrgkanis, arXiv:2002.07285). (ii) Staggered DiD and event studies (Callaway and Sant'Anna 2021) for switchers. (iii) Generalized synthetic control per target field, with donors matched on Prithvi embeddings, soil and weather. (iv) Causal forest (grf) and R-learner CATE with weather and climate effect modifiers. Spatially blocked cross-fitting, spatial basis confounder adjustment (Gilbert et al. arXiv:2112.14946), negative-control outcomes and exposures, and landscape exposure mapping for spillovers such as rootworm pressure from the share of continuous corn nearby.
- L4 Bias and uncertainty layer. Prediction-powered inference (Angelopoulos et al., Science 2023) corrects proxy-label measurement error. A trial-observational hybrid estimator (Kluger, Owen and Lobell, arXiv:2112.13700) pools LTAR, the Bowles et al. 11-site long-term rotation experiments and land-grant trials. Lei-Candès weighted conformal intervals per field. Jin-Ren-Candès conformal sensitivity analysis gives the 'flip strength'. Overlap/positivity maps produce the 'evidence density' of each recommendation.
- L5 Climate transport. NASA NEX-GDDP-CMIP6 (0.25°, downscaled, SSP2-4.5 and SSP5-8.5, multi-model ensemble) is bias-corrected to PRISM and resampled to growing-season effect-modifier distributions per field. The CATE is integrated over the future distribution, with ensemble spread and an out-of-support flag. Frost-prone and heat-stress hotspots come from ECOSTRESS LST climatology and CMIP6 tails.
- L6 Decision layer. A farmer-weighted multi-objective utility (income from USDA AMS Market News prices, input costs, drought insurance value, soil, water, labour) gives a Pareto front over the status quo plus 3 or more alternative regimes. For programs, Athey-Wager policy trees give interpretable targeting rules for EQIP/CSP cost-share and crop-insurance discounts.
- L7 Farmer-facing explanation. A 'Your field and its twins' map. Plain-language cards for each strategy: why suggested, the evidence (number of twin fields and trials), benefits, information gaps (low overlap), and risks needing local review (low flip strength, out-of-support climate). An SMS/voice summary mode modelled on advisory-RCT delivery. Farmers can add their own records, such as yield-monitor files and soil tests. Each upload tightens their own conformal interval and adds to the gold-standard sample used for PPI.

## Impacts
- **Stop paying farmers to lose money: target cover-crop and rotation cost-share to fields where the per-field causal effect is positive or the soil and water benefit is large** (Farmers in USDA NRCS EQIP/CSP and state programs, and taxpayers): The average satellite-estimated cover-crop effect is a 5.5% corn and 3.5% soybean yield loss after 3+ years of use, but it varies strongly with soil quality, spring rain and mid-season temperature. Athey-Wager policy trees on per-field CATEs move dollars toward fields with a neutral-to-positive yield effect and high erosion or water benefit. That should reduce farmers giving up the practice after a bad first experience and raise environmental return per dollar.. Scale: Cover cropping was EQIP's top-funded practice for FY2014-2025 by dollars obligated. EWG counts about $126M in cover-crop payments in 2019 alone. Press coverage put the average corn penalty at about $40/acre. Our assumption, not a measured result: if targeting avoids the penalty on even a third of enrolled acres, that is tens of millions of dollars a year in avoided farmer losses, plus higher soil and water benefit per program dollar.. Evidence: Deines et al. 2023, Global Change Biology 29(3):794-807, doi:10.1111/gcb.16489 (90,000+ fields, causal forests, heterogeneity by soil and weather). Stetter, Mennig and Sauer 2022, Eur. Rev. Agric. Econ. 49(4):723-759 (individual agri-environment effects differ markedly from averages, with targeting gains). ERS chart of note https://www.ers.usda.gov/data-products/charts-of-note/114076. EWG EQIP data https://oembed.ewg.org/node/8271
- **Capture the rotation yield effect on the fields where it is largest, and know where it is shrinking with warming** (Continuous-corn and simple-rotation growers, especially in hotter and drier margins): Per-field CATE of 'corn after soy' or a more diverse regime versus the status quo, conditioned on temperature and rain and transported under NEX-GDDP-CMIP6. Satellite and trial evidence shows the corn rotation benefit falls in hotter years while the soybean benefit rises, so the best rotation for a field changes as the climate shifts.. Scale: The corn rotation benefit averages about 1.02 t/ha (about 16 bu/ac), ranging from 1.18 t/ha in the coolest quintile to 0.84 t/ha in the hottest. At about $4.50/bu that is roughly $55-85/acre on the fields where it applies. Across millions of continuous-corn acres that adds up to hundreds of millions of dollars a year that can be targeted.. - **Drought and flood resilience shown as a field-specific counterfactual, which insurers and lenders could price** (Farmers, the federal crop insurance program (RMA), ag lenders): Resilience outcome: the yield anomaly in IMERG/ECOSTRESS-defined drought and excess-moisture years under each regime, estimated with synthetic control on real twin fields. This gives evidence for premium discounts tied to measured resilience rather than to the practice alone.. County-level cover-crop adoption is associated with lower insurance losses from drought, heat and especially excess moisture. Federal crop insurance pays out billions of dollars in indemnities each year, so even small, well-targeted loss reductions are material.. ## Demo moment
A judge clicks a real Iowa field that has been continuous corn since 2010. The map dims, and 40 to 60 actual neighbouring fields light up: its 'statistical twins'. Each matches its soil, slope and pre-2014 satellite trajectory, and each switched to corn-soy-oat with clover around 2014. A synthetic-control panel animates two lines: this field's real HLS greenness, SMAP-HydroBlocks storm-response and yield, against its counterfactual built from the twins. The lines track each other before the switch and diverge after it. In the 2023 dry spell the twin-built counterfactual holds about X% more root-zone water and loses less yield. Each number comes with a conformal interval ('+9 to +21 bu/ac, 80%'), a count of the evidence behind it ('47 twin fields, 2 long-term trials'), and a 'flip strength' gauge ('a hidden factor would need to make switchers 2.3x likelier to succeed to reverse this'). The judge then drags the NEX-GDDP-CMIP6 slider to 2045 under SSP5-8.5. The corn rotation benefit visibly shrinks as heat rises while the soybean and clover benefit grows. Part of the band turns hatched and labelled 'beyond the evidence – local strip trial recommended', which is the honest-uncertainty requirement from the brief shown live. A final toggle zooms out to the county. A policy tree lists, in three readable lines, which fields EQIP should pay for which rotation, and estimates the soil and water benefit per dollar compared with today's first-come allocation.
